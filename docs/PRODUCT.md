# Product

## The idea

Most people know what they want. They don't know where to start. Sprint answers "start here" and keeps answering it.

The user types or says a goal: "I want to launch my website by Christmas." Sprint understands the deadline, builds a mission with milestones and short sprints, and shows one thing: the next sprint and a START button.

## The loop

```
GOAL      "Launch my website by Christmas"
PLAN      5 milestones · 18 sprints · explained in one sentence
SPRINT    "Define the purpose of the site" · 15 min
FOCUS     The dial. Nothing else.
COMPLETE  Ring closes. "You moved the goal forward."
REWARD    +1 sprint · +momentum · mission progress · achievements when earned
NEXT      One recommendation, with a reason. Or take a break.
MOMENTUM  Grows with meaningful work, cools slowly, never snaps to zero
```

## What Sprint is not

Not a to-do list, not project management, not a calendar, not a Kanban board, not a habit tracker, not a chatbot. There is no inbox and no backlog view. Everything is organised around *the next sprint*.

## Language

Use: Start sprint · Next · Pause · Complete · Mission · Milestone · Momentum · Focus · What next?

Never: "You failed", "You're behind", "You missed", streaks, points for opening the app, exclamation marks, emoji as UI.

A missed day reads: *"Life got in the way. I've spread the remaining sprints across the days you have left."*

## Rewards

- **Momentum (0–100)**: gain per completed sprint scales with length and depth, with diminishing returns near the top. Half-life of four days. Bands: Warming up, Building, Rolling, Strong, Peak.
- **Levels**: from lifetime focused minutes (Starter → Vanguard). Honest, slow, unmanipulative.
- **Achievements**: first sprint, first milestone, mission complete, deep focus, comeback (returning after five quiet days counts double), consistent week, strong day, ten hours. Awarded once, shown quietly.

## Adaptive planning

Missed sprints roll forward on the next open. Running out of time becomes "time's up", still counted, with a one-tap follow-up sprint. Long sprints can be split in two. Deadline changes re-spread the plan and explain themselves. Every sprint can be edited, moved, skipped or deleted. The user is always in control.
